Draw Faster: New Shapes, Smarter Snapping, and Feet & Inches

September 12, 2026

Most of what makes an editor feel good has nothing to do with its headline features — it's the small stuff you reach for a dozen times a session. This update is three of those: a faster way to start a new shape, a grid and a snap setting that finally don't have to agree with each other, and a way to read your plan in feet and inches without changing a single number in your code.

Drop in a shape without touching the code

Sometimes you don't want to author a new line, rectangle, circle, or polygon by hand — you just want one on the canvas so you can shape it by dragging. There's now a New Element menu right in the header's Edit tab: pick a shape, and it drops into whichever element you've selected (or the plan's own root, if nothing is), ready to resize, restyle, and reposition however you like.

It's deliberately generic — the four basic shapes the language itself is built from, not a catalog of furniture. Anything more specific than that is exactly what you already have code and AI for.

Grid and Snap, finally two separate switches

Turning on the background grid used to also turn on snapping, whether you wanted it or not, and there was no clean way to set a snap distance that didn't match the grid's own spacing. Both of those are gone now: Grid and Snap are two independent buttons in the header, each with its own flyout, each with its own size. Want a fine visual grid but coarse snapping? Want snapping on with no grid showing at all? Both are one click away now, and neither setting has any opinion about what the other one is doing.

The grid's own flyout also grew the two settings that were previously code-only: whether it renders behind your shapes or in front of them as a translucent overlay, and how see-through that overlay is.

See it in feet and inches, without changing a thing

Planagonia's plan language is — and stays — metric under the hood, the same way it always has been: real-world units, unambiguous for a person or an AI to write. But not everyone thinks in meters, so the header now has a Units toggle that switches the scale bar and any dimension labels on your plan to feet and inches on the fly. Nothing about your plan's own code changes when you flip it — it's purely how the numbers are displayed to you, so a plan written by anyone, in any unit preference, still reads the same to everyone else.

Want to try any of this? Open the app — everything above is already live, no update needed on your end.

None of these change what a plan fundamentally is in Planagonia — still a small program, still something an AI can write for you and you can still shape by hand. They're just three fewer moments where the editor made you work around it instead of with it.
