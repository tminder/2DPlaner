The Toolbar We Redesigned Three Times in One Afternoon

September 5, 2026

Most software doesn't get to visibly change its mind. We do — Planagonia is small enough, and moves fast enough, that we can rebuild something the same day we start disliking it. This week that happened to the row of buttons across the top of the app: we redesigned it three separate times before landing on the version that's live now, and each attempt taught us something the last one hadn't.

Take one: everything behind three menus

The toolbar had grown the ordinary way software grows: one button added at a time, in whatever order they were built, with no grouping and no visual hierarchy at all. Rename, Delete, three flavors of Export, and a whole separate sign-in flow, all sitting in a flat row. The first fix looked obvious — group them. We built a small menu bar: File, View, Account, each opening its own dropdown, closing on an outside click or Escape, only one open at a time. Tidier immediately, and every existing button kept its own id and its own click handler — only the wrapper around it changed.

Take two: dressing it up like a ribbon

Grouped wasn't the same as clear. A plain vertical list of text still asked you to read every label to find anything. So we rebuilt the inside of each dropdown as a small ribbon panel — an icon on top, a short label underneath, grouped with a thin divider, the same visual language a modern office suite uses for its own toolbar. The "show grid" option stopped being a line with a checkmark next to it and became an actual toggle button, pressed and highlighted when it's on, the same way a Bold button looks pressed in a word processor.

Take three: show almost everything

Here's the part we didn't expect going in: once every action had a proper icon and a short label, most of them didn't need to be hidden behind anything anymore. Planagonia only has about eight real toolbar actions total — nowhere near enough to justify a click just to reveal them. So we removed the menu layer for anything that was really its own, separate action: Rename, Import, Delete, the grid toggle, signing in — all of it sits directly in the toolbar now, all the time, icon and label together, nothing to open or close first.

One thing stayed behind a small flyout on purpose: Export. Rename and Delete are genuinely different actions competing for the same row; a plan file, a PNG, and an SVG are the same action — getting your plan out of the app — with three destinations. Grouping those three together isn't the "hide it behind a click" problem we'd just spent an afternoon undoing, it's exactly what a dropdown is actually good for. So Export got its one flyout back, and everything else stayed flat. The row is a little longer than before either way, and on a phone it simply wraps onto a second line — a trade we're happy with, since nothing is ever more than one glance and, at most, one hover away.

A real popup instead of browser prompts

Signing in used to mean answering two of your browser's own plain prompt() boxes, one for a username and one for a password — functional, but the kind of thing that makes a product look unfinished. That's gone now too. Signing in and creating an account both happen in an actual popup inside the app, built to match the same sign-in form already used on your account page, so it's the same flow and the same wording whichever way you get there — and a genuine error message if something goes wrong, instead of nothing at all.

A few other things that shipped alongside it

  • Planagonia now has a real, automated test suite — a growing set of checks that actually launch the app in a browser and interact with it, run before anything ships. It's the kind of infrastructure you don't see, but it's the reason we can rebuild something like the toolbar three times in a day and still be confident nothing else quietly broke along the way.
  • One of the app's built-in starter examples, previously a single house with one power line, is now a small electrical grid — three connected parcels, each with its own house, all wired to a shared distribution cabinet, styled entirely through a handful of named, reusable style definitions rather than repeating the same colors on every shape. A good look at what that feature is actually for once a plan gets bigger than a single room.
  • The right-click menu on any object picked up a proper "Placement" submenu — options for keeping something inside its container, snapped flush to its edge, or set free of both, each shown with a checkmark for whichever is currently active, worded in plain terms instead of the underlying property names.
Want to see where it landed? Open the app — the toolbar at the top is exactly what's described here, live right now.

We're not precious about a first attempt here, or a second one — if a real look at something reveals it isn't quite right, we'd rather rebuild it the same day than leave it as-is until "later." Thanks for reading, and for putting up with a toolbar that occasionally changes shape underneath you.
