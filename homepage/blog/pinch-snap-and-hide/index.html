Pinch, Snap, and Hide: The Editor's Biggest Update Yet

September 7, 2026

A plan is never finished in one pass — you sketch the shell, add furniture, hide a layer to see what's underneath, resize a couch, nudge it onto the grid, and start again from your phone on the couch. This update is about making all of that feel natural, not just possible.

Layers, the way you'd expect them to work

A real plan is a stack of concerns — the room shell, the furniture, the wiring, the plumbing — and sometimes you want to see just one of them at a time. Planagonia now has a proper layers panel: expand any group, and hide or show it with one click. Hide the seating in a camper van build and the water tank hidden underneath it is suddenly visible, exactly where a real installer would need to see it. Reordering works the same way — drag a layer up, and it comes forward in the drawing, the same way it would in any design tool.

Finding — and starting — a plan, properly

Opening or starting a plan used to mean a small dropdown listing names. It's now two dedicated screens, one for each — and every plan shows as a real, live preview of what's actually in it, not just a label. Starting from scratch, picking one of the built-in examples, or going back to something you saved last week are all one click away, and you can actually see what you're picking before you commit to it.

A workspace that resizes with you

The code on the left and the drawing on the right now share a draggable divider, so you can give either side more room depending on what you're doing — writing a longer description, or looking closely at a dense layout. It remembers where you left it the next time you open the app.

The editor finally works properly on a phone

This is the one we're most excited about. Touch support wasn't an afterthought bolted onto the mouse-driven editor — it now rides the exact same gestures a mouse or trackpad already used:

  • Pinch to zoom, with two fingers, the way you'd expect on any map or photo app.
  • Hold a shape to open its right-click menu — no right mouse button needed.
  • Drag a shape's own corner to resize it — a small square handle appears on whichever piece of furniture or room you've selected, and dragging it grows or shrinks the shape directly, on both a screen and a trackpad. Circles get one handle on their own edge instead, for resizing the radius.

Sketch a room shell on your laptop, then pull out your phone on site to double-check a measurement or nudge a wall — all of it now works the way you'd actually reach for it.

Things line up, if you want them to

Turn on the background grid, and dragging or resizing anything now snaps it to that grid automatically — no more eyeballing whether two walls are actually aligned. It's tied directly to the same grid setting you already use to show the grid at all, so the two never disagree with each other about what "aligned" even means.

Want to try any of this? Open the app — everything above is already live, no update needed on your end. Bring it up on your phone and give the new touch gestures a go.

None of this changes what Planagonia fundamentally is — a plan is still a small program you (or an AI on your behalf) can write and read. What's changed is how much the editor around that program now feels like a real design tool, on whatever device you actually have in hand.
