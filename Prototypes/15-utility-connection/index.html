2DPlaner prototype: utility connection plan

A different kind of plan on purpose: a property (grundstueck, a polygon via shared corners, D-018), a house (haus, a plain rect, a child of the property), and a power line (stromanschluss, a 3-point polyline running from the street to the house) — the first prototype to model a site/infrastructure plan rather than a room's interior. Core and interactivity-module.js are otherwise identical to 14, copied unchanged: drag/select/connect/hover/ the context menu are genuinely domain-agnostic and needed zero changes for a completely different subject.

Two naming changes from a syntax review (D-033), both first applied here: the keyword is now element, not node — "node" carries a graph/network connotation of "a point," which fits a bare corner like ecke_1 but not this same primitive used as a shaped, styled, child-bearing thing like haus. "Element" is neutral on both counts, matching how the word is already used in XML/HTML/SVG for exactly this range of roles. And allowUnrealisticPolygons is now allowSelfIntersectingPolygons — the old name implied general realism-checking; only self-intersection is ever actually checked. Prototypes 01–14 keep the old names; they're historical snapshots, not rewritten.

Why no new module for the power line: a plain dashed polyline already says everything this plan needs — core has no built-in idea of "power line" any more than it has one of "wall" or "rug" (D-013), but nothing here needed new *rendering behavior* the way star-tool's (12/13/14) actual star shape did. A module earns its existence by teaching the renderer something it doesn't know, not by existing for a plan's sake — this plan just didn't need one.

connection haus_anschluss haus: drag haus and the connection point stays flush, still D-014's plain rigid propagation. Drag haus_anschluss itself, though, and it now slides along whichever wall it's actually resting on instead of dragging the whole house — a first real slice of D-032's "outside, attached" placement mode, no longer just a design on paper. Motion perpendicular to the wall is ignored; motion along it is clamped to that wall's own span (try dragging past a corner — it stops there with a message, doesn't jump onto a different wall or drag the house). Shift+drag still means "ignore all of this, move just me" — now doubling as an escape hatch from the wall constraint, not only from rigid propagation.

Select haus_anschluss or haus for a × icon at the wall to disconnect them, drag the point away, then drag it back near the wall and use the + icon to reattach — it snaps onto the wall exactly, correcting just the one coordinate that's off. This needed a real fix to interactivity-module.js: 08's original adjacency/icon system only ever knew about rects (bounding boxes were computed for shape: "rect" elements only), so a bare position-only point like haus_anschluss could never appear in it at all — selecting it showed no icons, connected or not. Fixed by treating any shapeless position-only element as a degenerate, zero-size box, with dedicated adjacency/snap math for the point-vs-rect case (a point has no extent to align, just one coordinate to zero out against the nearest edge it's already aligned with) — and by always moving the point on reconnect regardless of which side was selected first, since snapping a whole house onto a small attachment point would be the wrong side to move.

Bug found immediately after that fix, from actually trying it: with the icon rendered exactly at the point's own position (correct geometrically — that is the contact point), every click meant to grab and drag haus_anschluss instead hit the icon sitting on top of it and re-fired connect/disconnect — the drag never started. Invisible on a rect like haus (plenty of clickable area away from its small icon), total on a point with nothing else to click. Fixed by rendering the icon offset ~0.35m from the point, away from the rect, along whichever edge it's nearest — enough to clear both the icon's and the point's own hit radius, still visibly at the attachment site.
